About Jay

Two generations of Fairfield County real estate

Jay Cannone learned the business at his father's dinner table, earned his license at 19, and never left the county he grew up in.

Raised in the business

You might say that real estate is a family tradition for Jay Cannone. He grew up in Trumbull, Connecticut, where he has lived since 1976, as the son of John Cannone, one of Fairfield's premier real estate professionals. Home sales and mortgages were tableside chat, and Jay was raised on the lessons his father spent a lifetime developing.

Jay followed his father's footsteps into the business, earning his real estate license and beginning his career at the age of 19. Nearly four decades later, he is a Senior Sales Vice President and Luxury Properties Specialist at William Raveis's Trumbull office, representing home sellers and buyers across Fairfield County and New Haven County.

Family still comes first. Jay balances his career with his wife and children the same way his father did, and he treats clients the way he treats neighbors, because in Trumbull they usually are.

"There's no secret to how you succeed in real estate. It's very simple. You must work hard and be straight with your clients. As long as you follow that simple lesson, everything else will fall into place."

John Cannone, Jay's father, as quoted on jaycannone.com

Giving back

"On December 18th, 2020, my oldest son, John, passed away at the age of 29 from a drug overdose. He had been struggling with addiction for 12 years. Since then, I've made it my mission to help other families going through similar challenges with their loved ones. I want to offer support, understanding, and guidance to those dealing with addiction. By sharing my own experience, I hope to raise awareness about the dangers of drugs and prevent more tragedies."

"Though the pain of losing John will always be with me, I find comfort in knowing that I can make a difference and bring hope to others facing similar struggles. Please feel free to reach out to me anytime if you or someone you know is dealing with a loved one who suffers from addiction."

Jay Cannone, from a note published on jaycannone.com
